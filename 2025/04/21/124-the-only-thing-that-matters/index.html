124: The Only Thing That Matters

21 April 202521 เมษายน 20252025年4月21日

This entry is in English only.บันทึกนี้มีแต่ภาษาอังกฤษ此篇仅有英文。

So, I’m scrolling X a couple days ago, and this comedian’s up on stage, killing it.

He goes:

There are only two things you need to worry about in life: either you’re healthy or you’re not.

If you’re not healthy, you need to worry about two things: how to get better or how to get worse.

If you know how to get better, you’re good. If not, you worry about living or dying.

If you’re living, no worries. If you’re dying, you worry about heaven or hell.

Heaven? You’re set. Hell? Well, then you have problems.

It’s hilarious, but it’s also deep as hell. It’s like Hamlet’s “to be or not to be,” but with a punchline. Life’s complicated, sure, but at the end of the day, it boils down to one thing: your health. If you’ve got it, you’re free. If you don’t, good luck thinking about anything else.

I didn’t always get this. I’m the guy who’s always go-go-go—meetings, projects, late-night philosophy rants. But a few weeks ago, life threw me a curveball that made me see things crystal clear. Let me tell you about the centipede bite that turned my world upside down.

The Bite That Bit Back

It started innocently enough.

I’m in my kitchen, minding my own business, when I feel this sharp sting on my right foot. I look down, see a small scratch, and think, “Eh, probably nothing.” Big mistake. By evening, my foot’s throbbing like someone’s hammering nails into it. The pain’s deep, burning, like it’s clawing at my bones. I hobble to the hospital, where a young doc gives me a shot and says, “You’ll be fine.”

Spoiler: I wasn’t.

By the next day, my foot’s swollen to twice its size, looking like a lumpy, angry balloon.

Walking’s a nightmare—every step feels like stepping on glass. I try to tough it out, but the pain’s relentless. I can’t sleep, sit still, or do anything but grit my teeth and curse. I’m pacing my mom’s room at 3 a.m., trying to distract myself, but it’s useless. The pain’s got me in a chokehold. I rub my foot, smoke some weed to take the edge off, but it’s like throwing a pebble at a tsunami. Nothing works.

Back to the hospital I go. This time, they admit me, hook me up to an IV, and pump me full of painkillers.

For 18 hours, I’m lying there, half-awake, half-dreaming, with this dull, gnawing ache that won’t quit. My foot’s still swollen, still screaming, and I’m starting to wonder if this is my new normal. It was the kind of pain that makes you question everything—your choices, your strength, your will to keep going.

When Health Fails, Nothing Else Matters

Those long, brutal nights gave me a lot of time to think. And what hit me hardest was how fast everything else in my life became irrelevant. I had a packed week—trips to manage project programs, client meetings, even a little downtime planned to recharge. But with my foot fucked up, none of that mattered. I couldn’t work, couldn’t travel, couldn’t even focus on a simple phone call.

All my big plans, all my “important” goals—poof, gone. Because when your body’s screaming, your brain’s got no room for anything else.

It wasn’t just the physical toll.

The mental hit was just as bad. I’m a guy who thrives on getting shit done—writing, speaking, building smart cities. But lying there, I couldn’t think straight. My mind was a fog, trapped in this loop of pain and frustration. It made me think about folks who live with chronic pain or illness every day.

I used to be quick to judge, to think, “Just push through it.” But now? I get it. When pain’s your constant companion, it changes you. It humbles you. It makes you see how fragile this whole “life” thing really is.

What Pain Taught Me

So, what did I learn from this shitshow? A few things I’m carrying with me, and I think you should too.

1. Expect the Unexpected

Life’s a sneaky bastard.

You can plan every detail, but one tiny centipede can blow it all to hell. I had my week mapped out, but that bite forced me to cancel everything. The lesson? Always have a backup plan. Or a backup to your backup. Flexible schedules, understanding colleagues, a little wiggle room in your life — these are your lifelines when shit hits the fan. Because trust me, it will.

2. Pain’s a Brutal Teacher

I’ve always been the type to power through pain, physical or otherwise.

But this bite taught me that pain’s not just something to ignore—it’s a signal, a red flag waving in your face. It’s your body saying, “Yo, something’s wrong, fix it.” Ignoring it only makes things worse. That pain made me slow down, focus on healing, and appreciate the simple act of walking without wincing. It also made me more careful—now I’m checking every corner of my house like it’s a crime scene. Pain sucks, but it’s got lessons if you listen.

3. Health Is Everything

This is the big one. Health isn’t some optional extra you can put off till you’re “less busy.” It’s the foundation of your entire life. Without it, you can’t work, can’t dream, can’t be there for the people you love. Those days in pain showed me that all my achievements, all my hustle, mean jack shit if my body’s not on board. Health is the vehicle that carries you through life. If it breaks down, you’re stranded.

Zooming Out: Why Health Rules All

Let’s get philosophical for a sec, because you know I can’t help myself.

Why is health the ultimate deal?

Because it’s the only thing that lets you live life on your terms. When you’re healthy, you can chase your goals, laugh with your friends, take care of your mom, or just enjoy a damn good cup of coffee.

But when you’re not, everything stops. It’s like the comedian said—if you’re healthy, you don’t have to worry about anything. If you’re not, good luck thinking about anything else.

And health isn’t just about your body.

It’s your mind, your emotions, your spirit.

When I was in pain, I couldn’t think clearly, couldn’t focus on anything but the throbbing in my foot. It was a reminder that mental clarity and emotional stability are just as crucial as physical strength. Stress can make you sick, and sickness can stress you out—it’s a vicious cycle if you don’t break it.

But here’s the good news: we’ve got some control over this.

We can eat better, move our bodies, get enough sleep, and listen when our bodies tell us something’s off. We can build resilience, not just in our muscles but in our minds, so we’re ready for life’s curveballs. And we can learn from pain, using it to grow stronger, wiser, more careful.

Keep It Simple: Take Care of Yourself

So, what’s the big takeaway?

Prioritize your health. Make it your number one, non-negotiable, can’t-skip-it priority. Because without it, nothing else works. Eat real food, go for a walk, hit the gym, or just take a nap when you need it. And don’t ignore the little signals—those aches, those twinges. They’re trying to tell you something.

Life’s unpredictable, and shit will go wrong. But if you take care of your health, you’ll be ready to roll with the punches. Like that comedian said, if you’re healthy, you’ve got nothing to worry about.

If you’re not, well, you’ve got a whole lot on your plate. So, take care of yourself, folks. Your health is your superpower—don’t let it slip away.
